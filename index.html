Your brain is dumb, color is a lie, and there's no such thing as "brown"

  • Straight in. Every second before 4:00 counts toward the vote.

Color doesn't exist.

Light has a wavelength. That's all it has.

  • Say it flat. No hedging.
  • Visible light is a sliver, about 380 to 700 nanometres, between infrared and ultraviolet.

Your eye writes down three numbers.

(every screen in this room is lying to you)

  • Three kinds of cone squash a whole spectrum into three numbers. Say cones, not rods and cones: rods don't see color.
  • Different spectra with the same three numbers look identical. That's how RGB screens work.
  • The screens aside is the first thing to cut if the run is long.

Your brain makes up the rest.

Every color you've ever named was your head pretending to be the world.

  • The thesis. Everything after this is evidence.
  • Draft line: say it in your own words. Naming a color passes off something that happens in your head as something out in the world.

Orange is named after the fruit.

English had the fruit for a century before anyone called a color orange.

  • Evidence: the words drift over time too.
  • The fruit is in English by about 1380. The first "orenge colour" is in Elizabeth of York's wardrobe accounts in 1502.
  • Before that, red stretched to cover it.

That's why redheads are red.

(so are robin redbreasts. they're fossils.)

  • Red for hair is late Old English, and "redhead" goes back to 1256.
  • Never say red fox or red squirrel: both were named after orange existed.

Spanish files them under red too.

pelirrojo, petirrojo

(the RAE defines red hair as "a very bright blond")

  • Don't say Spanish lacked the word. It had naranjado by 1351.
  • Petirrojo is peto (bib) plus rojo, not pecho.
  • Check the DLE's entry for rojo before saying the blond line. It came from a search snippet.

There's no such thing as brown.

It's orange in a bright room.

(the proof needs a dark room and ten minutes, and you vote in a minute)

  • Evidence: the same light changes color with whatever's around it.
  • The title's promise. One line, then move on.
  • If challenged: brown is a related color, it only appears next to something brighter. A dim orange light alone in the dark is orange, never brown.
  • Technology Connections did it properly ("Brown; color is weird"). Point anyone who wants the proof there.

Purple isn't real.

  • Evidence, and the strongest yet: your brain invents a color that no light has.

Every wavelength you can see.

And a line your brain made up.

  • The answer to the cliffhanger. The horseshoe is every wavelength you can see, 380 to 700 nanometres, in order.
  • Click: the straight edge. Its official name is the line of purples, and no single wavelength looks like anything on it.
  • Your brain sees violet at one end and red at the other, draws a line across the gap, and makes up colors to fill it.
  • Evidence: purple is the color your brain invents outright.
  • If challenged: the real CIE 1931 chromaticity diagram is lopsided, and this is its idea tidied into a horseshoe. The straight edge really is officially named the line of purples.
