Color doesn't exist.

(light has a wavelength. that's all it has.)

  • Say it flat, over the prettiest rainbow anyone here has seen. No hedging.
  • Visible light is a sliver, about 380 to 700 nanometres, between infrared and ultraviolet.

Your eye writes three numbers.

Hofer et al., Journal of Neuroscience, 2005
  • These are real cones, in eight living human eyes, photographed through adaptive optics. Blue, green and red dots are the short, medium and long cones.
  • Three kinds of cone squash a whole spectrum into three numbers. Say cones, not rods and cones: rods don't see color.
  • Look how uneven they are. Same species, same eyes, normal color vision, and one person is mostly green while another is almost all red. Their L to M cone ratios run from about 1:1 to 16:1, and they all agree on yellow.
  • The blue cones are a small minority in everyone, and about the same in all eight.
  • Different spectra with the same three numbers look identical. That's how every screen in the room works.

Light is a line.

Your brain bends it into a horseshoe.

Then fills the gap with purple.

  • Light is a line. Every wavelength you can see, in order, violet at one end and red at the other. Past violet is ultraviolet, past red is infrared, and you can't see either.
  • Click: your brain bends the line into a horseshoe, so violet and red end up near each other.
  • Click: then it fills the gap with purple. Purple is your brain mixing the two ends of the line. No single wavelength looks like anything on that bar.
  • The bar has an official name: the line of purples.
  • If challenged: this is the idea of the CIE 1931 chromaticity diagram, tidied into a horseshoe. The real one is lopsided.

We're all colorblind.

Gemini North and Hubble, M. H. Wong et al.
  • Jupiter, both halves at the same moment. The top is infrared, from Gemini North: it glows where the clouds are thin and heat gets out. The bottom is ultraviolet, from Hubble: the Great Red Spot goes dark.
